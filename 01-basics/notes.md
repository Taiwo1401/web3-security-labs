### 1. What is Blockchain?
Blockchain is a decentralised digital ledger that records transactions or information in blocks linked together using cryptography. It operates across multiple computers called nodes, allowing participants to maintain and verify shared records without relying entirely on a central authority. Its structure makes unauthorised modification difficult and promotes data integrity and transparency.

### 2. Why Is Blockchain Difficult to Modify/Change?
Blockchain is difficult to modify because of cryptographic hashing, the linking of blocks, decentralisation and consensus mechanisms. Each block has a unique hash and contains the hash of the previous block, creating a connected chain. Changing information in an earlier block changes its hash and breaks the connection with subsequent blocks. Other network participants can detect invalid changes and reject them according to the network's consensus rules.

### 3. Cryptographic Hashing
Cryptographic hashing converts input data into a fixed-length digital fingerprint using a mathematical algorithm. In blockchain, each block has its own hash, which helps verify data integrity and detect unauthorised modifications. For example, SHA-256 produces a 256-bit hash (64 hexadecimal characters), and even a small change in the original data produces a significantly different hash.

### 4. Linking of Blocks
Blockchain consists of interconnected blocks, with each block containing transaction data, its own hash and the hash of the previous block. This creates a chronological chain where every block is linked to the one before it. If information in an earlier block is modified, its hash changes, breaking the expected connection with subsequent blocks and making the alteration detectable.

### 5. Decentralisation
Decentralisation means that control and responsibility are distributed among multiple participants rather than being managed by one central authority. In blockchain, computers called nodes maintain copies of the ledger and participate in validating transactions. This makes it difficult for an individual to secretly modify transaction records because changes must comply with the network's rules.

### 6. Consensus Mechanism
A consensus mechanism is the process through which participants in a blockchain network agree on valid transactions and new blocks. Two common mechanisms are Proof of Work (PoW), where miners use computational power to validate and add blocks, and Proof of Stake (PoS), where validators stake cryptocurrency to participate in network validation. Consensus helps maintain agreement and security across the network.

### 7. Why Do Hackers Target Blockchain?
Hackers target blockchain because it involves valuable digital assets, financial transactions and sensitive information. Their objectives may include stealing cryptocurrency, exploiting smart contract vulnerabilities, obtaining private keys, manipulating transactions and disrupting network operations. Common attack methods include phishing, Sybil attacks, 51% attacks, double-spending and smart contract exploits.
